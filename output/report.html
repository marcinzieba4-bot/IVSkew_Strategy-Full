VolVue 90-day IV skew · 9 SPDR sectors × top 20 · 3-week baskets ·

High-skew dips are the edge. Shorting on skew is the drag.

Every 3 weeks, each of 9 sectors contributes at most one name per group: up to 18 longs (G1 + G3) and 18 shorts (G2 + G4). Only the long mean-revert group (high skew, pullback already deep) beats its sector ETF by a wide margin. The short side loses money in absolute terms and drags the market-neutral book down to a low-risk, low-return profile.

Risk: the 18 long / 18 short book

The book has 36 fixed slots (9 sectors × 4 groups). Each slot holds 1/36 of capital, so the book runs at 1× gross and 50/50 long/short when every slot is filled. A slot with no qualifying stock sits in cash. The long book and short book rows each show their own 18 slots on their own capital.

VaR and CVaR are per 3-week basket (5% worst). Capture ratios are the average basket return in up and down SPY periods, divided by SPY's. Longest drawdown is time spent below the previous peak.

Stock-picking skill: return vs own sector ETF

This strips out market direction. Longs are measured as stock minus its sector ETF; shorts as sector ETF minus stock. It shows whether the skew signal picks the right names inside a sector.

Control: does skew matter, or is it just dip-buying?

This runs the same dip rule (≥ 8% below the 60-day high, deepest name per sector) in each skew bucket. All buckets carry the same survivorship bias, so the gap between them isolates what skew adds.

The monotonic climb from low to high skew is the real signal. The "any skew" row is close to G3 because the deepest dips usually carry high skew anyway, so a large part of G3's absolute return is dip-buying in names we already know survived.

Caveats

  • Survivorship bias. The universe is today's top-20 holdings, applied back to 2018. Buying dips in names that later became mega-caps flatters every long bucket, and G3 most of all. The sector-relative and control tables are the fairer read.
  • 9 sectors: XLK, XLY, XLV, XLI, XLC, XLB, XLE, XLP, XLU. CTRA and IPG are excluded because they have no price history after their mergers. EA is carried at its last price after it went private.
  • The skew thresholds (4.0 / 5.0 vol pts = 0.04 / 0.05) split the sample roughly 38% / 38%. Sector medians vary (XLK 4.2, XLI 5.1), so a percentile-based threshold may be a sensible next test.
  • Short costs (borrow, dividends paid) are not modelled beyond the 10 bp per side. Outlier skew prints outside [−10, 40] are dropped. WBD's current skew of 19 reflects its pending deal, not a normal hedging signal.
