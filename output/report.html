VolVue 90-day IV skew · 9 SPDR sectors × top 20 · 3-week baskets ·

Keep the skew longs, hedge with the sector ETF, rebalance every 3 weeks.

Every 3 weeks, each of 9 sectors contributes at most one name per group: up to 18 longs (G1 + G3) and 18 shorts (G2 + G4). Only the long mean-revert group (high skew, pullback already deep) beats its sector ETF by a wide margin. The short side loses money in absolute terms and drags the market-neutral book down to a low-risk, low-return profile.

Which way to run it

The picks are the same throughout: the 18 long slots (G1 + G3, one per sector). What changes is the short leg (none, sector ETF, SPY, or the old single-stock shorts), whether the longs are stock or calls, and whether the hold is 3 weeks or 1 month. Empty slots earn T-bills. Sharpe and Sortino here are on returns in excess of T-bills.

Notional-matched calls: calls on the same dollar notional as the stock slot. The premium is about 4% of notional (ATM) or 2% (30-delta), so it behaves like a small, capped position. Short SPY 0.5× / 0.75× / beta-matched: SPY short sized at half, three-quarters, or each stock's 120-day beta times the long notional. Calls + SPY puts: ATM calls on the slot notional plus long ATM SPY puts on 50% or 100% of that notional. ATM puts on shorts: ATM puts on the G2/G4 short picks, priced from VolVue's ATM put IV. The 36-slot book holds ATM calls on the 18 longs and ATM puts on the 18 shorts. ATM calls + short SPY 0.5×: ATM calls on 1× or 2× the slot notional, plus a short in SPY shares worth half the call notional. That is roughly delta-neutral, so it behaves like a long-volatility position: it pays time decay and profits from big moves. Delta-matched calls: calls on notional ÷ delta, so the position starts with the same exposure as holding the stock. Calls are priced with Black-Scholes on VolVue's ATM call IV plus a 3% spread on the premium, and held to expiry at the end of the basket. The 30-delta calls also use ATM IV because VolVue has no wing vols. That slightly overprices them, so their results are conservative.

Long single-stock calls + short SPY calls: sensitivity to SPY call skew

These rows buy delta-matched calls on each stock and sell SPY calls with the same dollar delta, so each slot starts delta-neutral. SPY OTM calls trade at a lower IV than ATM because of index skew. The main table assumes the 30-delta SPY call is 2 vol points below ATM. The result depends heavily on that assumption: each vol point is worth about 4–5% of CAGR.

SPY put timing: hold puts only when a rule is on

Each rule is checked on the signal day using past data only. When it is on, ATM SPY puts are held for that basket. The first table shows how the puts did when each rule was on versus off, in % of put notional per basket. The second shows the effect on the books. Follows the hold toggle above.

Rules: SPY put IV in the bottom 25% / 50% of its past year · IV minus 20-day realised vol in its bottom 25% · SPY 30-day skew in its bottom 25% · SPY below its 50 / 200-day average · "calm top" = within 2% of the 1-year high with IV in its bottom 25%. With 9 rules × 2 holds and only 20–75 baskets per rule, treat single wins with suspicion. Only a rule that works at both holds and in both halves of the sample is worth trusting.

Risk detail: the original 18 long / 18 short stock book (3 weeks)

The book has 36 fixed slots (9 sectors × 4 groups). Each slot holds 1/36 of capital, so the book runs at 1× gross and 50/50 long/short when every slot is filled. A slot with no qualifying stock sits in cash. The long book and short book rows each show their own 18 slots on their own capital.

Control: does skew matter, or is it just dip-buying?

This runs the same dip rule (≥ 8% below the 60-day high, deepest name per sector) in each skew bucket. All buckets carry the same survivorship bias, so the gap between them isolates what skew adds.

The monotonic climb from low to high skew is the real signal. The "any skew" row is close to G3 because the deepest dips usually carry high skew anyway, so a large part of G3's absolute return is dip-buying in names we already know survived.

Caveats

  • Survivorship bias. The universe is today's top-20 holdings, applied back to 2018. Buying dips in names that later became mega-caps flatters every long bucket, and G3 most of all. The sector-relative and control tables are the fairer read.
  • 9 sectors: XLK, XLY, XLV, XLI, XLC, XLB, XLE, XLP, XLU. CTRA and IPG are excluded because they have no price history after their mergers. EA is carried at its last price after it went private.
  • The skew thresholds (4.0 / 5.0 vol pts = 0.04 / 0.05) split the sample roughly 38% / 38%. Sector medians vary (XLK 4.2, XLI 5.1), so a percentile-based threshold may be a sensible next test.
  • Short SPY calls carry uncapped upside risk if the market rips while the chosen stocks lag. The worst 3-week basket for the long-ATM / short-30Δ-SPY book was about −10%.
  • Option results are modelled, not traded. There are no historical option quotes, so each call is priced from VolVue's ATM call IV at entry. Real fills on single-name options can be wider than the 3% spread assumed, especially for OTM strikes.
  • Short costs (borrow, dividends paid) are not modelled beyond the 10 bp per side. Outlier skew prints outside [−10, 40] are dropped. WBD's current skew of 19 reflects its pending deal, not a normal hedging signal.
